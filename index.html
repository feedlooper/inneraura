About me

Hi, I’m Gurleen.

I’m a breathwork facilitator based in Vancouver. My work is rooted in helping people slow down, soften their nervous system, and reconnect with what is happening inside the body — not from a place of pressure, but from curiosity and care.


Breathwork has been one of the most beautiful tools I’ve found for creating space. Space to feel, release, process, rest, and return to yourself. My sessions are warm, grounded, and beginner-friendly, with time before and after to talk through what came up.

  • For people feeling stressed, emotionally heavy, anxious, stuck, or disconnected.
  • For anyone curious about breathwork but wanting a safe and gentle introduction.
  • For couples or small groups wanting a meaningful shared experience.
The practice

What is breathwork?

Breathwork is an intentional breathing practice that uses the breath to shift energy, bring awareness into the body, and support emotional release. You do not need any experience — just a willingness to be present with yourself.

〰

Release stress

Breathwork can help move built-up tension through the body and create a feeling of lightness, calm, and spaciousness.

◌

Reconnect inward

The practice gives you quiet time to notice your body, emotions, and inner world without needing to explain or fix everything.

✦

Feel grounded

Many people leave sessions feeling clearer, softer, more present, and more connected to themselves.

Questions

Before you come in.

A few simple things to know before booking your first breathwork session.

Book now

Do I need experience?

No. Sessions are beginner-friendly and I’ll walk you through everything before we begin.

What should I wear?

Wear loose, comfortable clothing. You’ll be lying down for the breathing portion of the session.

Should I eat beforehand?

It’s best to eat lightly and leave about two hours between your last meal and the session.

Where are sessions held?

Sessions are currently offered in Vancouver. You’ll receive the full address and arrival instructions after booking.
